               Staircase Wear Analysis - Walking Through History

                                      Summary

                  Stair wear patterns in historic buildings can provide valuable archaeological evidence regard-
             ing the building’s age, usage and traffic patterns throughout history. In this paper, we develop a
            comprehensive mathematical framework to analyze wear patterns and provide archaeologists with
              quatitative tools for historical interpretation.

                    First, we establish a non-destructive measurement methodology including physical, chemical
          and biological measurements. Physical measurements involve 3D scanning of stairs and monoc-
              ular depth estimation. Chemical measurements include isotope analysis and mineral composition
               testing. Biological measurements involve the study of microbial colonies. Then, we personally col-
              lected top-down photos of stairs from ancient buildings. Using depth map estimation methods,
         we drew heatmaps and obtained wear data.

               Following data collection, we address the challenge of quantifying wear volume from discrete
             point cloud data. We employ Delaunay triangulation to transform the theoretical volume integral
              into a weighted summation of local triangular volumes, thus accurately calculating spatial wear
            volume.

               Second, we build a Daily Foot Traffic Model based on the Archard equation. We take Ar-
           chard Adhesive Wear Model and Abrasive Wear Model both into consideration. Besides, we
            use Bayesian Inversion Framework to calculate the construction time to provide essential param-
                eter.

                 Third, we establish a Wear Distribution Model to determine movement patterns and simul-
             taneous occupancy levels. The model analyzes wear patterns to deduce whether people moved in
              single file or side by side, and whether there were predominant directional preferences.

               For age estimation, we employ multiple approaches: C14 dating method for wooden stair-   微信公众号：数学模型              cases, weathering degree analysis for stone staircases, and Bayesian inversion.We constructed a
           weighted evaluation model of three representative Chemistry Weathering Indecies to evaluate
             the weathering condition of stone, providing information about its age.

             To detect repairs or renovations, we analyze age distribution discontinuities using anomaly de-
              tection methods like the Z-score. We also verify the consistency of wear patterns with available
               historical information. For material source verification, we use the Archard Wear Model to cal-
              culate wear coefficients and perform chemical composition analysis to trace the origin of materials.
        We analyze usage patterns by examining the distribution of wear depth. By calculating the kurtosis
             of the distribution, we determine the usage pattern of stairs.     微信号：MATHmodels
                  Finally, we perform a sensitivity analysis on the key parameters of our model to evaluate its
             responsiveness. The results demonstrate strong robustness.

            Key Words:  Stair Wear, Archard Equation, Depth Estimation, Weathering Degree Model,
            Bayesian Inversion

        1  Introduction


           1.1  Problem Background

             The constant wear on stairs, especially in historic buildings, is a thought-provoking phenomenon.
            For example, many ancient buildings, such as temples and churches, show more wear in the center
             of the stairs than on the sides due to long-term use. The wear not only reflects the use of the stairs,
             but can also provide some important clues to archaeologists about the history of the building and
          how the stairs were used. The study of stair wear can help archaeologists speculate on the age of
             the building, the frequency of use, restoration, and past traffic patterns.

                 Different patterns of stair use and wear reflect people’s behavior, such as whether they walked
           up and down the stairs alone or with many people walking side by side. Our research aims to de-
            velop a mathematical model for archaeologists to better understand the history of building use and
              to infer information such as the frequency of using stairs, directional preference and the number of
            people using stairs at the same time. This process is important for the investigation and conserva-
              tion of historic edifices, offering a scientific foundation for archaeological endeavors and serving
             as a valuable reference for restoration and preservation initiatives.

             The major problems(tasks) dealt with in this paper will be specified in Section 1.3.


           1.2  Literature Review

             Our research focuses on the wear on stairs caused by prolonged foot traffic. However, there
                is little direct research on this topic. Previous research mainly focused on material wear such as
                rail wear, gear wear, or some other wear in precision instruments. Previous research pointed out
               that wear can be categorized into five types: adhesive wear, abrasive wear, corrosive wear, erosive
             wear, and fretting wear [1]. We primarily focus on adhesive wear and abrasive wear, and believe
               that the wear on stairs mainly belongs to abrasive wear, with adhesive wear being secondary [2].
             Therefore, Archard’s wear equation [3] can effectively link the wear volume to the usage conditions
             of the steps. Furthermore, some researchers have suggested that carbon-14 isotope dating can help            determine微信公众号：数学模型the usage period of materials such as stone [4].  Additionally, some studies on using
           machine learning, specifically CNN models [5], to train data and obtain rail wear amounts have
            provided us with valuable insights.

           2.2  Assumptions

              Through a complete analysis of the problem, in order to simplify our model, we make the
             following reasonable assumptions.     微信公众号：数学模型              Assumption 1: The treading position on the stairs is normally distributed when a single
           person walks.

          As the number of steps increases, the distribution of treading positions approaches a normal distri-
              bution.

              Assumption 2: The time spent on each step by an individual is the same.

               People generally maintain a steady pace, especially when walking without significant external
              disturbances. In addition, the time spent on each step tends to be similar due to the coordinated       微信号：MATHmodels
           rhythm of movement, particularly during continuous stair climbing.

              Assumption 3: When multiple individuals walk side by side, their positions are symmet-
              rically distributed.

               People often adjust their walking positions to avoid crowding or collisions, naturally forming a
              pattern in which their positions are distributed symmetrically along the walking path.

2.3  Detailed Measurement Plan

   To analyze the wear patterns of stairs and make speculation about their usage, historical context,
and traffic patterns, we propose a detailed measurement plan. The following approach integrates
physical, chemical, and biological measurements to collect relevant data in a non-destructive,
low-cost, small-team, and minimal-tool measurement.


2.3.1  Physical Measurements

   Through physical measurements, we need to obtain the three-dimensional image of the staircase
to establish the coordinate system, material hardness, wear degree and material properties.


(1) 3D Scanning of Stairs with Laser Scanner
  We should use a laser scanner to scan the steps, capturing precise geometric data of the stairs [6].

   Data Collected:

    1. Three-dimensional spatial coordinates of the stairs.

    2. High-resolution surface details, including the surface roughness and micro deformation.

   Tool Required: Laser scanner (could be handheld or tripod-mounted).

    1. Preliminary analysis

4  Estimate the Age of the Staircase


    In this part, we will figure out the estimation of the age of the stairs. The reason for dedicating
a separate section to it is that the C14 dating method, weathering degree model, and Bayesian
inversion method used here have an insightful role in solving the subsequent problems.

   For wooden stairs, the construction year can be inferred using the radiocarbon dating method
(Carbon-14 dating).

   For stone stairs, the construction year can be inferred by analyzing the degree of weathering.
By selecting parts of the stairs that are less likely to be directly affected by foot traffic, such as the
riser, stringer, or lightly worn areas of the step tread, and examining the degree of weathering, the
construction year of the stone stairs can be accurately estimated.

           5.3  Analysis of Usage Patterns

                     If there is a small number of people walking over a long period, it can generally be assumed
               that people will follow the distribution of single-file walking and occasional walking side by side,
           which leads to localized deeper wear on the stairs.

               However, if a large number of people use the stairs over a short period, they will have to walk
              side-by-side, following a distribution pattern for multiple people walking together,which results in
            a flatter distribution.

                 Therefore, we can determine the type of walking pattern and assess the density of foot traffic
             over a short time based on the distribution.

             The kurtosis of the wear depth distribution is calculated to determine whether the stairwell
            experienced short-term high-intensity usage or long-term low-intensity usage:

                                                       µ4   微信公众号：数学模型Kurtosis =   −3,                                  (41)                                                σ4

               where: µ4 is the fourth central moment, σ is the standard deviation.

          A high kurtosis (> 3) indicates long-term low-intensity usage, while a low kurtosis (≤3) indi-
              cates short-term high-intensity usage.

def visualize_depth_scatter(points, depths, save_path=’scatter.png’):
    """Method A: Direct scatter plot visualization"""

def visualize_depth_image(points, depths,
                         save_path=’grid.png’,
                         resolution=256):
    """Method B: Grid interpolation visualization"""
    if not HAS_SCIPY:
        print("Scipy required for interpolation")
        return

    x_min, y_min = np.min(points, axis=0)
    x_max, y_max = np.max(points, axis=0)
    grid_x , grid_y = np.mgrid[x_min:x_max:complex(resolution),
                             y_min:y_max:complex(resolution)]
    grid_z = griddata(points , depths, (grid_x, grid_y),

              extent=(x_min, x_max, y_min, y_max),
              cmap=’jet’, aspect=’auto’)
    plt.colorbar(label=’Wear Depth’)
    plt.title(’Interpolated Wear Depth Map’)
